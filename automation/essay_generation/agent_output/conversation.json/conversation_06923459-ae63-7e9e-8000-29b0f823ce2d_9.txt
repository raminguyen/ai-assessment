Classical Conditioning in the Cats’ Behavior

Classical conditioning, first described by Ivan Pavlov, involves learning to associate a neutral stimulus with a significant one so that the neutral stimulus eventually elicits a response on its own (Pavlov, 1927). In this scenario, the sound of the can opener or the sight of Professor Catlove opening a can has become a conditioned stimulus (CS) for the cats. Operant Conditioning in Both Cats and Professor

Operant conditioning involves learning through consequences—behaviors are strengthened if followed by rewards or weakened if followed by punishment (Skinner, 1938). In this kitchen scene, operant conditioning operates on multiple levels.

For the cats: Their behaviors—purring, meowing, rubbing—may have been shaped by positive reinforcement. Social Learning in Feline Behavior

Social learning theory posits that organisms can learn new behaviors by observing others (Bandura, 1977). In multi-cat households like Professor Catlove’s, social learning may play a significant role. Younger or less experienced cats may observe older cats responding to cues such as the can opener or specific times of day.
